An Example of an APA-Style Paper

APA style refers to a set of standards put in place by the American Psychological Association for authors to use when submitting journal articles for publication. Standardization of text size, spacing, font, and referencing allows journal editors and instructors to focus on the content of an article or assignment rather than being distracted by formatting differences.

The first set of rules was created in 1929 and was laid out in just a few pages. Today, the APA Manual is published as a book that is more than 400 pages.

Running Head

Older versions of the APA Manual used to require a running head on every page. The running head was a shortened version of the title of the paper, and it was written in all capital letters. The 7th edition of the APA Manual now requires a running head only on papers submitted for professional publication but not on student papers.

Ask your professor if a running head is needed on your paper.

Page Number

Each page is numbered, including the title page, so that if the pages of the paper are separated, they can be easily put back in order.

Identifying Information

This block contains the following pieces of information:

  • Title of assignment. Check to see what your professors want. Some professors may want you to simply name your paper Assignment 1 while others may want you to create a more descriptive title.
  • Student name. This identifies the author or authors of the paper. Check to see if your professor also wants your student number included.
  • Affiliation. This appears on two lines. The first line is the name of your program and St. Lawrence College. The next line is your course code followed by the name of the course.
  • Professor’s name. This is needed so that if you submit your assignment by putting a printed copy in a drop box or if you forget it in a classroom, it can be put in the correct professor’s mailbox.
  • Assignment due date: Check to see if your professor wants the date to appear in a particular format.
Abstract

The abstract is a summary of your paper that is no more than 250 words. From the abstract, the reader must be able to tell the key points and key findings/conclusions of the paper. It is not simply an overview of the subjects that the paper covers; instead, it provides the important details from the paper.

If the paper describes an experiment, then the abstract needs to explain the hypothesis, method, and key findings of the experiment. (i.e., what you did, why you did it, and what you found.) If your paper is an essay, then your abstract needs to contain your overall argument, key points that support it, and your overall conclusions. Another way to think of it is like this: imagine that your essay is a movie. A “teaser” hints at what’s in the movie, but a “spoiler” gives away all the secrets. Make your abstract a “spoiler” for your paper.

Note: Not all programs/professors will require an abstract page.

Body of the Paper

Most of your paper will be paragraphs that appear in this section.

Key Features:

  • Paragraphs are double spaced
  • There is no extra space between paragraphs. The only indication that a new paragraph has started is the indent.
  • Paragraph indents are .5 inch/ 1.27 cm

Common Errors:

Some word processing programs use a different default spacing that automatically puts extra spaces before or after paragraphs. Be sure to change this. If you are not sure how to change the spacing, use the Help function in Word.

References

In APA style, references and citations are used rather than footnotes to indicate material that is taken from another source.

The reference page is essential to ensure that your assignment does not contain plagiarism. It allows your reader to look up the information that you used, and it gives credit to your original source for the words, ideas, statistics, or images that you have used.
