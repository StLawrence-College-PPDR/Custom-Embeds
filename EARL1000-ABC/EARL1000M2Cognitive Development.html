Cognitive Development

Exploring Piaget's 4 Stages of Intellectual Growth

Birth to 2 Years

Sensorimotor Stage

During this earliest stage, infants and toddlers acquire knowledge through sensory experiences and manipulating objects.

  • Object Permanence: Learning that things exist even when they cannot be seen.
  • Basic Actions: Sucking, grasping, looking, and listening.
  • Goal-Oriented Behavior: Moving from reflexive actions to intentional steps.
2 to 7 Years

Preoperational Stage

At this stage, kids learn through pretend play but still struggle with logic and taking the point of view of other people.

  • Symbolic Thought: Using words and pictures to represent objects.
  • Egocentrism: Difficulty seeing things from the perspective of others.
  • Language Development: Rapid growth in vocabulary and sentence structure.
7 to 11 Years

Concrete Operational Stage

Children begin to think logically about concrete events. They begin to understand the concept of conservation.

  • Conservation: Realizing that the amount of liquid in a short, wide cup is equal to that in a tall, skinny glass.
  • Inductive Logic: Reasoning from specific information to a general principle.
  • Reduced Egocentrism: Beginning to think about how other people might think and feel.
12 Years and Up

Formal Operational Stage

The final stage involves an increase in logic, the ability to use deductive reasoning, and an understanding of abstract ideas.

  • Abstract Thinking: Reasoning about hypothetical problems and moral issues.
  • Deductive Logic: Reasoning from a general principle to specific information.
  • Systematic Planning: Ability to plan for the future and consider "what if" scenarios.
