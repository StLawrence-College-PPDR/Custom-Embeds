Decoration

Indigenous women adorned moccasins through diverse techniques, such as using red ochre paint, fringe, ribbon, quillwork, and copper bells. The patterns on the moccasins frequently conveyed the heritage, community, or personal history of the maker or wearer.

2
Fabrication

Indigenous peoples employed distinct methods for crafting moccasins, yet there were commonalities in their approaches. Traditionally, women engaged in the moccasin-making process by cleaning, stretching, and sun-drying animal skins. Using a bone knife or a similar tool, they would carefully scrape the hides to achieve a consistent appearance and texture. Prior to crafting the moccasins, the skins were often treated through smoking, and the women skillfully stitched them together using sinew.

3
Creation

Typically, moccasins were created from a single piece of leather, featuring a seam running down the center from the back of the shoe. Nevertheless, various styles and types of moccasins existed. Some incorporated gathered cloth sewn at the toe, while others boasted a flatter design. The inclusion of side flaps, fur linings, high-cut designs (extending above the ankle), and low-cut varieties (reaching ankle length) depended on the preferences of the shoemaker and the intended purpose of the moccasin. For instance, fur linings served to provide warmth in colder climates, a feature unnecessary for those in warmer environments.
