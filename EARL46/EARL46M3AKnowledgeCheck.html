Knowledge Check: Legalities in Child Care

1. Which Act defines the regulations and requirements for a safe child care environment in Ontario?
  • The Child Care and Early Years Act (CCEYA)
  • The Ontario Health and Safety Act
  • The Child Day Care Privacy Act
Answer: The CCEYA specifies program requirements including space, ratios, and health/safety.
2. If a parent signs a permission form for a field trip, is the caregiver released from all liability?
  • Yes, the form acts as a total legal waiver.
  • No, if negligence can be proven, the caregiver is still liable.
Answer: Permission forms only demonstrate recognition of risk; they do not excuse negligence.
3. What is the legal obligation for reporting suspected child abuse?
  • Report it after consulting with the child's parents.
  • Report it only if you have physical evidence.
  • Report the suspicion and info to CAS promptly/immediately.
Answer: You must report "forthwith" if you have reasonable grounds to suspect harm or risk.
4. What are "reasonable grounds" for a suspicion of abuse?
  • Being 100% certain that abuse occurred.
  • What an average person with your training would suspect using honest judgment.
  • Hearing a rumor from another parent.
Answer: You do not need to be sure; you just need to exercise normal and honest judgment.
5. Under CFSA s.72(3), can you rely on a supervisor to report a suspicion on your behalf?
  • Yes, reporting through a chain of command is required.
  • No, the person who has the suspicion must report directly to CAS.
Answer: The duty to report is personal; you must not rely on anyone else to report for you.
6. What is the maximum fine for a professional who fails to report a suspicion of abuse?
  • $5,000
  • $50,000
  • $10,000
Answer: Professionals can be fined up to $50,000 and/or face two years in prison.
7. When is a professional's duty to report abuse NOT required due to confidentiality?
  • If the child asks you to keep a secret.
  • Only in a solicitor-client (lawyer) relationship.
  • If the information was shared during a private counseling session.
Answer: The duty to report overrides all provincial statutes except solicitor-client privilege.
8. A caregiver can only deny a parent access to a child if:
  • The parent is more than an hour late.
  • There is a written legal document (custody order, peace bond, etc.) on file.
  • The other parent says the visiting parent is dangerous.
Answer: You must see written legal documentation before denying a parent their custody rights.
9. If an impaired parent insists on driving away with their child, you should:
  • Physically block the car from leaving.
  • Release the child but call the police with the vehicle description and plate number.
  • Wait until the next day to report it to CAS.
Answer: Only a Court/CAS can withhold a child, but you should contact police to report an impaired driver.
10. What is "Vicarious Liability"?
  • The child's responsibility for their own injuries.
  • The employer's responsibility for the actions/torts of the employee.
  • The parent's responsibility to pay for daycare insurance.
Answer: Employers are responsible for employee actions because they control hiring and monitoring.
